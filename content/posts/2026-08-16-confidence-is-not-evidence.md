> Am I being told the truth?

I have a tendency to enjoy grandiloquence; if someone speaks with certainty and
flourished prose, I'm captivated[^1]. I often mistake this awe-inspiring
confidence for the delivery of truth.

<!-- more -->

Like many, I used to follow online _mentors_. And I would, sometimes for hours,
listen to their views.

There was something deeply comforting about thinking that someone, somewhere,
had found something so fundamentally true that it could apply to everyone,
everywhere.

Life is messy and complex; their confidence and certainty were reassuring.

---

And somewhen, I got lost: I started thinking about what I consumed. It became
harder to find certainty as I kept questioning the information I received.

> Am I being told the truth?

It opened my eyes to the idea that some mentors are actually _preachers_[^2].

They use poise and wit as ground for their facts. This attitude can be due to
actual expertise, but also to manipulation, incentive, or [Nobelitis].

**Confidence is not evidence.**

---

Complete expertise is rarely possible. I don't know everything, and neither do
you. We all have gaps in the map of our knowledge; we bridge them with
beliefs[^3].

The only path to expertise is to dedicate focused time to a topic. We must
consume data, arguments, counter-arguments, research, etc. Eventually we might
understand, become proficient, and even feel confident.

However, we must not confuse the extent of our knowledge.

**Confidence is not evidence, even inside our own head.**

---

It's known by now that LLMs are often [confidently wrong]. They are not truth
machines, but write as if they are.

> Am I being told the truth?

Even as experts, we should ask for sources. Think critically about what LLMs
tell us.

**Confidence is not evidence, even at a million tokens per second.**
